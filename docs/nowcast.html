Nowcast GDP from monthly data

Drop in a spreadsheet of monthly indicators and a quarterly target such as GDP. The page estimates a mixed-frequency dynamic factor model and tells you what the data say about the quarters not yet published, with standard errors, and which releases moved the estimate. Everything runs in your browser; your data are not uploaded anywhere.

1Data

A table with dates in the first column (or a column called date) and one column per series: CSV, TSV, or Excel. Daily, weekly, monthly and quarterly series can share one file or come in several; dates like 2024-03-31, 2024-03, 2024M03, 2024Q1 or Mar 2024 all work, and each series' frequency is read from its own dates. Leave a cell empty when there is no value — late starts, gaps and discontinued series are fine.

4What moved the nowcast

Compare with an earlier vintage of the data: the change in the nowcast splits exactly into the effect of each series' new releases (judged against what the earlier data expected) and of revisions to numbers already out. The model's parameters are held fixed.

The model. On standardized data, r factors follow a VAR(1) at the model's frequency (weekly or
      monthly); each series at that frequency is a factor loading times the factors plus its own noise; a series
      observed less often sits in its period's last week or month and loads on exactly the weeks or months of its period —
      four or five weeks to a month, thirteen or fourteen to a quarter — as the growth of the period's average level
      (Mariano and Murasawa's weights, 1, 2, 3, 2, 1 for a quarter of months, built the same way for any calendar) or as
      its average. Daily series are averaged to the model's periods. Estimation is the EM algorithm of Bańbura and Modugno (2014) with independent idiosyncratic
      errors, started from principal components; the news decomposition is theirs, computed exactly. The smoother
      underneath solves for the whole factor path as one sparse linear system — Cozy's ksmooth — and the
      model is Cozy's nowcast package, running here as WebAssembly.

    
The bands around the target's own figures are for the number that will be published: the uncertainty of the common
      component given the data, plus the parameters' (a parametric bootstrap — simulate data from the fitted model with the
      real data's gaps, re-estimate, re-smooth; Pfeffermann and Tiller), plus the series' own noise. The finer-frequency
      tracker has no published figures, so its bands are the common component's and the parameters'.
