Nowcast GDP from monthly data

Drop in a spreadsheet of monthly indicators and a quarterly target such as GDP. The page estimates a mixed-frequency dynamic factor model and tells you what the data say about the quarters not yet published, with standard errors, and which releases moved the estimate. Everything runs in your browser; your data are not uploaded anywhere.

1Data

A table with dates in the first column (or a column called date) and one column per series: CSV, TSV, or Excel. Monthly and quarterly series can share one file or come in several; dates like 2024-03-31, 2024-03, 2024M03, 2024Q1 or Mar 2024 all work. Leave a cell empty when there is no value.

4What moved the nowcast

Compare with an earlier vintage of the data: the change in the nowcast splits exactly into the effect of each series' new releases (judged against what the earlier data expected) and of revisions to numbers already out. The model's parameters are held fixed.

5Reproduce

The page runs the Cozy script below. Download it with the cleaned data and run it in Cozy (cozy nowcast_script.cz) or in the playground — it gives the same numbers, and it is the starting point for anything the page does not do.


  


  

    
The model. On standardized data, r factors follow a VAR(1); each monthly series is a factor
      loading times the factors plus its own noise; a quarterly series sits in its quarter's third month and loads on
      the months of its quarter, with the weights 1, 2, 3, 2, 1 (Mariano and Murasawa, for growth rates) or 1, 1, 1
      (for averages). Estimation is the EM algorithm of Bańbura and Modugno (2014) with independent idiosyncratic
      errors, started from principal components; the news decomposition is theirs, computed exactly. The smoother
      underneath solves for the whole factor path as one sparse linear system — Cozy's ksmooth — and the
      model is Cozy's nowcast package, running here as WebAssembly.

    
Standard errors measure the uncertainty of the common component given the data and the estimated parameters;
      they do not include parameter uncertainty or the series' own noise.
